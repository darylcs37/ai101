Urban Building-Material RF Model - Command Modern Operations (CMO)

Purpose

A production-oriented reference for modelling RF reflection, transmission, penetration, absorption, and diffuse scattering from urban building materials. It supports deterministic ray tracing, hybrid RF digital twins, and 3GPP-aligned outdoor-to-indoor (O2I) simulations from sub-6 GHz through mmWave.

Core principle: preserve complex field amplitude and phase for deterministic paths; convert to power only after coherent ray summation at the receiver.

Scope and model hierarchy

TierRepresentationUse caseKey limitation
0Opaque blocker / fixed lossRapid outdoor coverageNo path-specific reflections or O2I detail
1Single-interface Fresnel coefficientFacade reflection raysNo finite thickness or multilayer structure
2Finite lossy dielectric slabConcrete, brick, plain glass, timberMisses detailed coatings and reinforcement
3Multilayer transfer matrixCurtain wall, glazing, insulated facadeRequires layer data
4Explicit roughness/rebar/aperture modelCritical mmWave or forensic workComputationally costly
5Statistical O2I modelNetwork/system simulationNot building-specific

Use a hybrid model for city-scale RF twins: resolve geometry and dominant external rays deterministically, assign material archetypes or stacks to facades, then represent unresolved construction variation with calibrated stochastic uncertainty.

Energy partition

For incident power \\(P_i\\):

\[

P_i=P_{\mathrm{spec}}+P_{\mathrm{diff}}+P_t+P_a

\\]

where \\(P_{\mathrm{spec}}\\) is coherent specular reflection, \\(P_{\mathrm{diff}}\\) is diffuse scatter, \\(P_t\\) is transmitted power, and \\(P_a\\) is absorbed power.

For an ideal smooth, lossless interface:

\[

R+T=1

\\]

For realistic facade materials:

\[

R+T+A+S=1

\\]

Material electromagnetic properties

Represent a material with complex relative permittivity:

\[

\tilde{\epsilon}_r(f)=\epsilon_r'(f)-j\epsilon_r''(f)

\\]

or with conductivity explicitly represented:

\[

\tilde{\epsilon}_r(f)=\epsilon_r'(f)-j\left[\epsilon_r''(f)+\frac{\sigma(f)}{\omega\epsilon_0}\right]

\\]

\[

\omega=2\pi f

\\]

The complex propagation constant is:

\[

\gamma=\alpha+j\beta=j\omega\sqrt{\mu\left(\epsilon-j\frac{\sigma}{\omega}\right)}

\\]

The field in a layer is:

\[

E(z)=E_0e^{-\gamma z}=E_0e^{-\alpha z}e^{-j\beta z}

\\]

Material-only power attenuation through thickness \\(d\\) is:

\[

L_{\mathrm{bulk}}=8.686\alpha d\quad[\mathrm{dB}]

\\]

A pragmatic frequency-dependent fitting form is:

\[

\epsilon_r'(f)=af^b,\qquad \sigma(f)=cf^d

\\]

The frequency unit used in these fits must be stored with the coefficients. Material properties vary with composition, moisture, curing, temperature, coatings, and frequency; use distributions rather than immutable universal constants.

Local ray geometry and polarization

For ray direction \\(\hat{k}_i\\) pointing toward a facade and outward normal \\(\hat{n}\\):

\[

\cos\theta_i=-\hat{k}_i\cdot\hat{n}

\\]

Specular outgoing direction:

\[

\hat{k}_r=\hat{k}_i-2(\hat{k}_i\cdot\hat{n})\hat{n}

\\]

Construct local TE/s direction:

\[

\hat{s}=\frac{\hat{n}\times\hat{k}_i}{\|\hat{n}\times\hat{k}_i\|}

\\]

and local TM/p direction:

\[

\hat{p}_i=\hat{s}\times\hat{k}_i

\\]

Decompose incident electric field:

\[

E_{\mathrm{TE}}=\mathbf{E}_i\cdot\hat{s},\qquad E_{\mathrm{TM}}=\mathbf{E}_i\cdot\hat{p}_i

\\]

Antenna vertical, horizontal, or ±45° slant polarization is a global antenna reference. Reflection and transmission must use the local TE/TM basis at every interaction.

Fresnel reflection and transmission

For complex media 1 and 2, with complex intrinsic impedance \\(\tilde{\eta}\\):

\[

\tilde{\eta}=\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{\mu}}{\tilde{\epsilon}}}

\\]

Complex Snell's law:

\[

\tilde{n}_1\sin\theta_i=\tilde{n}_2\sin\theta_t

\\]

TE reflection:

\[

\Gamma_{\mathrm{TE}}=\frac{\tilde{\eta}_2\cos\theta_i-\tilde{\eta}_1\cos\theta_t}{\tilde{\eta}_2\cos\theta_i+\tilde{\eta}_1\cos\theta_t}

\\]

TM reflection:

\[

\Gamma_{\mathrm{TM}}=\frac{\tilde{\eta}_2\cos\theta_t-\tilde{\eta}_1\cos\theta_i}{\tilde{\eta}_2\cos\theta_t+\tilde{\eta}_1\cos\theta_i}

\\]

Reflected power fraction and reflection loss:

\[

R=|\Gamma|^2,\qquad L_{\mathrm{refl}}=-10\log_{10}(R)

\\]

At normal incidence:

\[

\Gamma=\frac{\tilde{n}_1-\tilde{n}_2}{\tilde{n}_1+\tilde{n}_2}

\\]

For air to a low-loss dielectric:

\[

\Gamma\approx\frac{1-\sqrt{\epsilon_r'}}{1+\sqrt{\epsilon_r'}}

\\]

For an ideal \\(\epsilon_r=4\\) dielectric, \\(\Gamma=-1/3\\), yielding \\(R=1/9\\), or about 11.1% reflection at the front interface. This does not represent the full finite-wall response.

Finite lossy slab model

A physical wall has two interfaces. For a slab of thickness \\(d\\), material layer 1 between media 0 and 2:

\[

k_{z,1}=k_0\sqrt{\tilde{\epsilon}_{r,1}-\sin^2\theta_i},\qquad k_0=\frac{2\pi f}{c}

\\]

Complex field transmission:

\[

T_{\mathrm{slab}}=\frac{t_{01}t_{12}e^{-jk_{z,1}d}}{1+r_{01}r_{12}e^{-j2k_{z,1}d}}

\\]

Complex slab reflection:

\[

R_{\mathrm{slab}}=\frac{r_{01}+r_{12}e^{-j2k_{z,1}d}}{1+r_{01}r_{12}e^{-j2k_{z,1}d}}

\\]

For air–slab–air:

\[

L_{\mathrm{pen}}=-10\log_{10}(|T_{\mathrm{slab}}|^2)

\\]

For different surrounding media, use normal Poynting-flux ratio:

\[

T_{\mathrm{power}}=\frac{\operatorname{Re}(Y_2)}{\operatorname{Re}(Y_0)}|T_{\mathrm{field}}|^2

\\]

A simple oblique bulk-distance approximation is:

\[

d_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{d}{\cos\theta_t},\qquad L_{\mathrm{bulk,oblique}}=8.686\alpha\frac{d}{\cos\theta_t}

\\]

Use it only as a fallback. The slab model is preferred because it includes coupling, refraction, absorption, phase, and repeated internal reflections.

Multilayer transfer matrix

For a realistic facade of \\(N\\) ordered layers, define for layer \\(i\\):

\[

\mathbf{M}_i=

\begin{bmatrix}

\cos(k_{z,i}d_i) & j\sin(k_{z,i}d_i)/Y_i\\

jY_i\sin(k_{z,i}d_i) & \cos(k_{z,i}d_i)

\end{bmatrix}

\\]

Then multiply in physical order:

\[

\mathbf{M}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{M}_i=

\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{bmatrix}

\\]

For incident admittance \\(Y_0\\) and exit-side admittance \\(Y_s\\):

\[

r=\frac{Y_0A+Y_0Y_sB-C-Y_sD}{Y_0A+Y_0Y_sB+C+Y_sD}

\\]

\[

t=\frac{2Y_0}{Y_0A+Y_0Y_sB+C+Y_sD}

\\]

\[

R=|r|^2,\qquad T=\frac{\operatorname{Re}(Y_s)}{\operatorname{Re}(Y_0)}|t|^2

\\]

\[

L_{\mathrm{pen}}=-10\log_{10}(T)

\\]

Use a multilayer model for glazing, air gaps, laminated glass, insulation, foil barriers, cavity walls, gypsum partitions, curtain walls, and building-integrated antenna structures.

Facade archetypes

Plain concrete and masonry

Treat concrete as a lossy, heterogeneous dielectric. Critical variables are thickness, water content, aggregate, reinforcement, exterior render, frequency, incidence angle, and surface roughness.

concrete_archetype:
  dielectric_model: complex_permittivity_frequency_fit
  moisture_states:
    dry: {weight: 0.20, parameter_set: lower_loss}
    nominal: {weight: 0.60, parameter_set: median}
    wet: {weight: 0.20, parameter_set: upper_loss}
  thickness_m:
    distribution: truncated_normal
    mean: 0.20
    std: 0.04
  transmission_model: finite_slab_or_multilayer
  reflection_model: fresnel_plus_roughness

Reinforced concrete

Reinforced concrete is a composite:

\[

L_{\mathrm{RC}}=L_{\mathrm{concrete}}+L_{\mathrm{rebar}}+L_{\mathrm{scatter}}+X_{\sigma,\mathrm{RC}}

\\]

Rebar regimeConditionModelling approach
Dense mesh\\(s\ll\lambda\\)Conductive screen or high-loss equivalent layer
Transitional grid\\(s\sim\lambda/2\\) to \\(\lambda\\)Explicit wire grid, periodic unit cell, or calibrated excess loss
Sparse bars\\(s\gg\lambda\\)Explicit bars only for critical paths
Unknown constructionRebar geometry unavailableHigh-loss class with broad uncertainty

At mmWave, reinforcement may be electrically large and can cause strong polarization dependence, localized leakage, diffraction, and spatially variable indoor coverage.

Plain and insulated glazing

double_glazing:
  layers:
    - {material: glass, thickness_m: 0.006}
    - {material: air_or_argon_gap, thickness_m: 0.012}
    - {material: glass, thickness_m: 0.006}

Use the multilayer transfer matrix to retain angle, polarization, phase, and cavity effects.

Low-E / IRR / solar-control glazing

Treat conductive coatings as thin sheet boundaries when electrically thin:

\[

Z_s\approx\frac{1}{\sigma_ct_c},\qquad Y_s=\frac{1}{Z_s}

\\]

For a sheet in free space at normal incidence:

\[

\Gamma_{\mathrm{sheet}}=\frac{-\eta_0Y_s}{2+\eta_0Y_s},\qquad T_{\mathrm{sheet}}=\frac{2}{2+\eta_0Y_s}

\\]

Insert the coating at its actual location in the glazing stack. Do not group all windows under “glass”: coated glazing may dramatically increase outdoor-to-indoor loss and external reflection.

Metal cladding, foil, and conductive panels

For a good conductor:

\[

\delta=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\omega\mu\sigma}}

\\]

If \\(d\gtrsim3\delta\\), bulk transmission through an intact panel is generally negligible. Treat it as an opaque reflector. Model actual coupling through seams, apertures, vents, doors, windows, cable penetrations, perforations, panel edges, and diffraction paths.

Surface roughness and diffuse scatter

A practical roughness smoothness criterion is:

\[

\sigma_h<\frac{\lambda}{8\cos\theta_i}

\\]

Coherent-specular reflection reduction:

\[

\rho_{\mathrm{coh}}=\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{4\pi\sigma_h\cos\theta_i}{\lambda}\right)^2\right]

\\]

\[

\Gamma_{\mathrm{coh}}=\rho_{\mathrm{coh}}\Gamma_{\mathrm{smooth}}

\\]

\[

P_{\mathrm{spec}}=|\Gamma_{\mathrm{coh}}|^2P_i

\\]

Allocate coherence-reduced reflected power into a calibrated diffuse term:

\[

P_{\mathrm{diff}}=\zeta\left(|\Gamma_{\mathrm{smooth}}|^2-|\Gamma_{\mathrm{coh}}|^2\right)P_i

\\]

A directional diffuse-scattering lobe can be represented by:

\[

p(\theta_s)\propto\cos^m(\theta_s)

\\]

Larger \\(m\\) produces a narrow lobe around specular direction; smaller \\(m\\) yields broader scatter. At mmWave, facade texture that is irrelevant at sub-6 GHz can substantially change coherent and diffuse energy partition.

Statistical outdoor-to-indoor model

For system-level O2I modelling:

\[

PL_{\mathrm{O2I}}=PL_{\mathrm{out}}+PL_{\mathrm{tw}}+PL_{\mathrm{in}}+X_{\sigma,\mathrm{O2I}}

\\]

Where \\(PL_{\mathrm{out}}\\) is outdoor loss to the exterior, \\(PL_{\mathrm{tw}}\\) is external-wall loss, \\(PL_{\mathrm{in}}\\) is indoor propagation loss, and \\(X_{\sigma,\mathrm{O2I}}\\) captures building-to-building variation.

For facade fractions \\(p_i\\), combine materials in linear transmission domain:

\[

PL_{\mathrm{tw}}=PL_{\mathrm{angle}}-10\log_{10}\left[\sum_i p_i10^{-L_i/10}\right]

\\]

Never average material losses directly in dB:

\[

PL_{\mathrm{tw}}\ne\sum_i p_iL_i

\\]

Measurement and calibration

Use a calibrated free-space reference path:

\[

H(f)=\frac{S_{21,\mathrm{material}}(f)}{S_{21,\mathrm{reference}}(f)}

\\]

\[

L_{\mathrm{trans}}(f)=-20\log_{10}|H(f)|

\\]

Approximate reflection and transmission metrics, after proper calibration and de-embedding:

\[

R(f)\approx|S_{11}(f)|^2,\qquad T(f)\approx|S_{21}(f)|^2

\\]

  frequencies:

    - band_edges

Fit reflection and transmission jointly where possible:

\[

\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\sum_{f,\theta,\mathrm{pol}}\left[w_R|R_{\mathrm{pred}}-R_{\mathrm{meas}}|^2+w_T|T_{\mathrm{pred}}-T_{\mathrm{meas}}|^2\right]

\\]

Quality assurance

acceptance_checks:
  physics:
    - TE and TM coefficients converge at normal incidence
    - passive smooth slabs satisfy R_plus_T_less_than_or_equal_to_one
    - increased conductivity or wall thickness does not increase transmission
    - good conductors reflect strongly with negligible bulk transmission
    - no passive material produces net gain
  numerical:
    - complex_square_root_branch_decays_in_lossy_media
    - transfer_matrix_is_stable_for_lossy_thick_stacks

Common failure modes

  • Treating a front-interface Fresnel coefficient as total loss through a wall.
  • Adding bulk attenuation but omitting entry and exit coupling.
  • Treating low-E, IRR, laminated, tinted, and ordinary glazing as one class.
  • Averaging wall losses in dB across concrete and glass regions.
  • Applying global antenna polarization directly to a local TE/TM interaction.
  • Adding diffuse scatter after already counting the lost specular power as absorption.
  • Modelling reinforced concrete as homogeneous dry concrete without an uncertainty term.
  • Treating metal cladding as a finite moderate-loss wall rather than an opaque reflector with seams and openings.
  • Combining deterministic façade transmission with full empirical O2I wall penetration loss.
  • Returning one “building penetration loss” number without frequency, angle, polarization, facade class, or uncertainty bounds.
